Task

Find and treat causes

Important: First exclude life-threatening causes

An acute change in cognition is a medical emergency until proven otherwise. Before or alongside cognitive testing, complete an ABCDE assessment: observations (NEWS2), capillary glucose, oxygen saturation, pain assessment, bladder scan for retention, bowel chart, medication chart review and a drug/alcohol history (Trust teaching; SIGN 157 – first consider acute life-threatening causes: hypoxia, hypotension, hypoglycaemia, intoxication or withdrawal).

Bedside cause checklist

Delirium is almost always multifactorial. Identify and manage all possible underlying causes or combinations of causes (NICE CG103 1.7.1). Use the Trust teaching mnemonic DELIBERATE or PINCH ME (Appendix B) as a checklist.

  • Pain
  • Infection
  • Nutrition
  • Constipation
  • Hydration
  • Medication
  • Environment and electrolytes

PINCH ME (alternative bedside checklist): Pain · Infection · Nutrition · Constipation · Hydration · Medication · Environment / Electrolytes.

Minimum investigation set

Bedside

  • Full observations (NEWS2), capillary glucose, SpO₂
  • Bladder scan (retention)
  • Bowel chart / PR if constipation suspected
  • Pain assessment (incl. non-verbal scale)
  • Hydration / nutrition (MUST)
  • Sensory check (ears, glasses)

Bloods

  • FBC, U&E, LFT, bone profile (calcium), magnesium, glucose, CRP, TFT, B12/folate
  • Blood cultures if sepsis suspected
  • Drug levels where relevant (e.g. lithium, digoxin, phenytoin)
  • Blood gas if hypoxia / hypercapnia suspected

Other, as indicated

  • Urinalysis only to support a clinical picture of UTI – a positive dipstick alone in an older person is not diagnostic (asymptomatic bacteriuria is common); MSU if symptomatic
  • Chest X-ray if respiratory signs
  • ECG (also required before any antipsychotic – see 12.6)
  • CT head if focal neurology, head injury, on anticoagulants, fall with head strike, or no cause found
  • EEG if non-convulsive status considered; LP if CNS infection suspected
  • Alcohol/drug history; withdrawal scoring

Medication review

Review the whole drug chart with pharmacy. Particular culprits: anticholinergics (e.g. oxybutynin, tolterodine, tricyclics, hyoscine, chlorphenamine, cyclizine, promethazine), opioids (especially tramadol, codeine accumulation in renal impairment), benzodiazepines and Z-drugs (intoxication and withdrawal), gabapentinoids, corticosteroids, antihistamines, antiparkinsonian agents, digoxin, lithium, and recently stopped medicines (withdrawal). Use an anticholinergic burden scale [Trust to confirm which – e.g. ACB scale]. Where medication is required for behavioural disturbance, choose agents with the lowest appropriate anticholinergic burden (SG feedback).

Treat what you find

Sepsis pathway; correct hypoxia, hypotension, dehydration, electrolyte disturbance (Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺); manage pain; relieve retention and constipation; thiamine (Pabrinex) if alcohol misuse or malnutrition; manage withdrawal (section 13.3); stop or reduce culprit drugs; treat the medical/surgical condition.
