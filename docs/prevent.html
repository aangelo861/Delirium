Task

Prevent delirium

Four admission risk factors

Within 24 hours of admission (and at pre-operative assessment for elective surgery) every adult must be assessed for the four NICE risk factors (CG103 1.2.1):

  1. Age 65 years or older
  2. Cognitive impairment (past or present) and/or dementia – if in doubt, use a validated measure (e.g. AMTS, MoCA) and ask the family/carer about baseline function
  3. Current hip fracture
  4. Severe illness (a clinical condition that is deteriorating or at risk of deterioration)

Important: If any one risk factor is present

If any one of these is present, the person is at risk of delirium and must:

  • have a baseline 4AT recorded (section 9);
  • have the multicomponent prevention package started within 24 hours (section 7);
  • have a medication review by an experienced healthcare professional (SIGN 157);
  • be observed at every opportunity for changes in risk factors or indicators (CG103 1.2.2, 1.5.1).

Additional recognised precipitating and predisposing factors that should heighten vigilance (from the Trust teaching material and SIGN 157): frailty; sensory impairment (vision, hearing); polypharmacy (particularly anticholinergics, opioids, benzodiazepines); infection; dehydration; malnutrition; constipation; urinary retention; pain; sleep deprivation; alcohol misuse; previous delirium; depression; multiple bed moves.

Multicomponent prevention bundle

Delivered by a multidisciplinary team trained and competent in delirium prevention, tailored to the individual's needs and care setting, and started within 24 hours of admission (NICE CG103 1.4). Ensure the same team cares for the person where possible and avoid moving people within and between wards or rooms unless absolutely necessary (CG103 1.4.1–1.4.2).

Column headings from the policy table: Domain / Actions (NICE CG103 1.4.3–1.4.10; SIGN 157).

Cognitive impairment / disorientation

Appropriate lighting and clear signage; a clock (24-hour in critical care) and calendar visible; re-orientate – explain where they are, who you are, what your role is; cognitively stimulating activities (e.g. reminiscence); facilitate regular visits from family and friends (open/extended visiting for carers).

Dehydration / constipation

Encourage oral fluids; fluid balance chart; consider subcutaneous or IV fluids if necessary; take advice on fluid balance in comorbidities (e.g. heart failure, CKD); bowel chart and management of constipation.

Hypoxia

Assess for hypoxia; optimise oxygen saturation if clinically appropriate.

Infection

Look for and treat infection; avoid unnecessary catheterisation; infection-control procedures.

Immobility / limited mobility

Mobilise soon after surgery/admission; encourage walking (with walking aids where needed and accessible); range-of-motion exercises for those who cannot walk.

Pain

Assess for pain – look for non-verbal signs, particularly in people who cannot communicate (e.g. Abbey Pain Scale, PAINAD); start and review appropriate pain management.

Medication

Review all medicines for type and number; rationalise; minimise anticholinergic burden (see 10.3), opioids, benzodiazepines and other sedatives; check for withdrawal risk.

Poor nutrition

Follow the Trust nutrition policy; ensure dentures fit properly and are used; assistance with meals; red trays / protected mealtimes.

Sensory impairment

Resolve reversible causes (e.g. impacted ear wax); ensure hearing aids and glasses are available, working and worn.

Sleep disturbance

Avoid nursing or medical procedures during sleeping hours if possible; schedule medication rounds to avoid disturbing sleep; reduce noise and light at night; avoid sedative-hypnotics.

Bladder and bowel

Regular toileting; avoid catheters where possible; screen for retention.

Important: No prophylactic medication

Antipsychotics and other drugs must not be used prophylactically to prevent delirium (SIGN 157 – insufficient evidence of benefit; benzodiazepines markedly increase the odds of delirium).

Consider the use of a structured ward-level prevention bundle (e.g. the NHS Scotland TIME bundle – Think/exclude triggers, Investigate and intervene, Management plan, Engage and explore – or an equivalent Trust care plan) [Trust to confirm local bundle].
