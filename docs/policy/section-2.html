Section 2 of 19

Key messages

  1. Think delirium. Anyone aged 65+, with cognitive impairment or dementia, with a current hip fracture, or with severe illness is at risk. Assess risk within 24 hours of admission and observe for change at every opportunity (NICE CG103 1.1–1.2).
  2. Delirium is a medical emergency. It is associated with increased mortality, longer length of stay, institutionalisation and incident dementia. Look for and treat the cause.
  3. Hypoactive delirium is the most commonly missed. A quiet, withdrawn, sleepy patient who is not eating or drinking may be delirious.
  4. Use the 4AT. A score of 4 or more indicates probable delirium; 1–3 indicates possible cognitive impairment; 0 makes delirium unlikely (though it does not exclude it).
  5. If you cannot tell whether it is delirium, dementia or both – manage as delirium first (NICE CG103 1.6.3; NICE NG97).
  6. Prevent it. A tailored multicomponent package (orientation, hydration, nutrition, mobilisation, sleep, sensory aids, pain control, medication review, avoiding bed moves) reduces the incidence of delirium by roughly a quarter to a third.
  7. Medication does not treat delirium. Pharmacological treatment should not be used to treat delirium itself. Medication should only be considered where non-pharmacological measures have been ineffective or are not feasible, and the patient's distress or behaviour presents an immediate risk or prevents essential treatment.
  8. If medication is needed: oral first; haloperidol at the lowest clinically appropriate dose (0.25–0.5 mg), repeat only after reassessment, cumulative dose above 2 mg/24 h in older adults requires senior review, for the shortest possible time (usually ≤ 1 week), and never in Parkinson's disease or dementia with Lewy bodies. Benzodiazepines are not recommended for routine management.
  9. Reassess daily and stop as soon as clinically appropriate. Antipsychotics started for delirium should not routinely be continued at discharge.
  10. Involve families and carers and give them written information. Communicate the diagnosis to the GP at discharge (NICE QS63 statements 4 and 5).
