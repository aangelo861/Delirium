Task

Monitor after medication

Urgent: Escalate immediately

Escalate any NEWS2 trigger, respiratory rate < 10, SpO₂ < 90 % (or below target), systolic BP < 90 mmHg, or unresponsiveness immediately.

Escalate immediately: RR < 10, SpO₂ below target, SBP < 90, unresponsive, new rigidity/fever (NMS), acute dystonia, dysphagia.

Resuscitation equipment, oxygen, flumazenil and procyclidine must be accessible.

Monitoring frequency

Standard monitoring

At 15–30 minutes after the dose and then at least hourly until there are no further concerns (NICE NG10 standard).

Increased-frequency monitoring

Increase to every 15 minutes for at least the first hour if: the patient appears asleep or oversedated; the BNF or Trust maximum has been exceeded; IM route was used; the patient has significant physical illness, has had alcohol or illicit drugs, or has been physically restrained.

What to monitor and document

Following administration of any antipsychotic or benzodiazepine, monitor and document (Appendix E):

  • respiratory rate
  • oxygen saturation
  • blood pressure
  • pulse
  • temperature
  • level of consciousness (ACVPU / sedation score)
  • degree of agitation/distress (target behaviour)
  • adverse effects, including extrapyramidal symptoms (dystonia, rigidity, tremor, akathisia), dysphagia, hypotension, oversedation

Daily review and stopping
  • The indication for ongoing antipsychotic (or benzodiazepine) treatment should be reviewed at least daily and the drug discontinued as soon as clinically appropriate. The review should be documented by the responsible medical team and the ward pharmacist.
  • Antipsychotics should not routinely be continued following resolution of delirium. Continuation of antipsychotics started in hospital for delirium, and their unplanned continuation after discharge, is a recognised patient-safety problem in which it is often unclear who is responsible for review.
  • If, exceptionally, an antipsychotic must continue at discharge, the discharge summary must state: the indication; that it was started for delirium; the dose; a stop date or review date (within 1–2 weeks); and who will review it (named GP / Care of the Elderly / Older Adult Mental Health / liaison team). See Appendix F.
  • Any patient still requiring an antipsychotic for delirium after 7 days must be discussed with Care of the Elderly or Liaison Psychiatry and the diagnosis reconsidered (section 13.6).

If medication continues at discharge

If, exceptionally, an antipsychotic must continue at discharge, the discharge summary must state: the indication; that it was started for delirium; the dose; a stop date or review date (within 1–2 weeks); and who will review it (named GP / Care of the Elderly / Older Adult Mental Health / liaison team).
